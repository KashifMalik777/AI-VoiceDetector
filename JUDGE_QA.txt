How to use this: these are the questions judges actually ask, with answers you
can say out loud. Numbers here are the CURRENT measured values. Where a number is

Quick fact sheet (memorize these):
  - Backbone: wav2vec2-XLS-R-300m, truncated 24 -> 7 layers, 101.3M params.
  - Runs as INT8 ONNX (102 MB) on CPU.
  - Window 4 s, hop 1 s, 16 kHz mono. First verdict ~4.4 s.
  - Latency (demo laptop): ~669 ms/window, RTF 0.167, +330 ms hop headroom.
  - Deployed model = augmentation-hardened "v4": seen-generator EER 0.75%.
  - Unseen-generator (measured, pre-aug head): 0.42% familiar / 17.13% novel VC.
  - OOD EER 8.4% = EXPECTED value from layer-7 truncation (not a fresh measurement).
  - Data: ~830 real / ~384 fake, 104 speakers, 9 generators, English + Hindi.
  - Fusion weights: neural 0.55, codec 0.25, speaker 0.20. EMA 0.6. K-of-N 3/6.


================================================================================
A. PROBLEM & IMPACT
================================================================================

Q. What problem does this solve?
A. Voice cloning is cheap and convincing — 30–60 seconds of audio is enough to
   clone someone. Fraudsters use it to call banks and authorize transfers in a
   customer's or executive's voice. Humans can't tell by ear. We detect synthetic
   / converted voice on the LIVE call, in time to hold a risky transaction.

Q. Who is the user?
A. A bank fraud-desk officer or relationship manager on a live call, plus the
   bank's compliance/audit function. It's an advisory tool for a human, not an
   autonomous blocker.

Q. Why is this urgent now?
A. The tools crossed the "good enough to fool a person over a phone line" line in
   the last two years, and they're free. The attack scales; the defense hasn't.

Q. Isn't caller-ID / OTP / a security question enough?
A. Those verify the CHANNEL or KNOWLEDGE, not the VOICE. Caller-ID is spoofable,
   OTPs get social-engineered ("read me the code you just got"), and security
   answers are often public. None of them notice that the voice itself is
   synthetic. We add the missing layer: the biometric signal on the wire.


================================================================================
B. THE MODEL / ML
================================================================================

Q. What model do you use and why?
A. Meta's wav2vec2-XLS-R-300m — self-supervised, pre-trained on 436k+ hours across
   128 languages, so it already understands the acoustics of speech. We don't
   fine-tune the whole thing; we freeze it and train a small linear head on top.

Q. What is "layer truncation" and why does it help?
A. The full model has 24 transformer layers. Deep layers encode meaning/language,
   which OVERFITS to specific words and languages. Early-to-middle layers capture
   acoustic physics — vocal-tract dynamics and vocoder artifacts. We keep only the
   first 7 layers. That cuts params from ~318M to 101.3M (~3x less compute) and,
   per the truncation literature, drops expected out-of-domain EER from ~16.9% to
   ~8.4%. Shallower features generalize better to generators we've never seen.

Q. You only train a linear head? Isn't that too simple?
A. The backbone already did the hard representation learning on 436k hours. A
   linear probe on frozen features is a standard, strong technique — and it means
   we retrain in MINUTES on the small head instead of hours on the whole network,
   so we can retrain every time new attack data arrives. The numbers (0.75% EER)
   show the features are separable with a linear boundary.

Q. How is the head trained?
A. For each 4-second window we mean-pool the layer-7 features to a 1024-dim vector
   and fit a logistic-regression head (P(fake) = sigmoid(w·h + b)) with L2
   regularization. Torch, full-batch, a few hundred epochs — seconds on GPU.

Q. Why INT8 quantization?
A. To run on a CPU in real time. FP32 ONNX is 405 MB; dynamic INT8 is 102 MB (4x
   smaller) and fast enough to stay well inside the 1-second hop. Banks won't put a
   GPU on every call server.


================================================================================
C. EVALUATION & HONESTY (judges love these)
================================================================================

Q. What's your accuracy?
A. We don't lead with accuracy — it's misleading on imbalanced data. We report EER,
   minDCF, actDCF, C_llr, and FAR@1%FRR. Headline: equal error rate 0.75% on a
   language-matched, speaker-disjoint English+Hindi test.

Q. What is EER?
A. Equal Error Rate — the operating point where the false-accept rate (a fake
   passed as real) equals the false-reject rate (a real flagged as fake). Lower is
   better; 0.75% means at the balanced threshold we're wrong under 1% of the time
   on this test.

Q. Is your test set leaking? How do you prevent it?
A. Two protocols. (1) Speaker-disjoint split: a speaker never appears in both train
   and test — we hold out ~25% of speakers per side. Detectors love to secretly
   learn speaker identity instead of "fakeness"; without this the numbers are
   fiction. (2) Leave-one-generator-out: we can pull an entire fake generator out
   of training and test only on it — the honest "unseen generator" number.

Q. What's the difference between minDCF and actDCF?
A. minDCF is the cost at the best possible threshold (oracle). actDCF is the cost
   at a threshold we fix IN ADVANCE. The GAP between them is deployment risk — a
   system can separate classes beautifully (low minDCF) yet be uncalibrated and
   useless at a real threshold (actDCF near 1.0). Our gap is small, so the scores
   are usable, not just separable. We Platt-scale to keep them calibrated.

Q. Why English AND Hindi? Why matched?
A. Deployment is Indian banking calls. And the benchmark is language-matched ON
   PURPOSE: if real is one language and fake is another, the model learns to
   separate LANGUAGE, not synthesis — a confound. We caught exactly this early (an
   English-real vs Bengali-fake run gave a meaningless result) and threw it out.

Q. What is the "OOD EER 8.4%" on your slide?
A. That's the EXPECTED out-of-domain EER from the layer-7 truncation research — an
   architectural expectation, not a fresh measurement. Our measured generalization
   is the leave-one-generator-out number: 0.42% against a familiar TTS family and
   17.13% against a novel cross-lingual voice-conversion model.

Q. 0.75% seems too good. Are you overfitting?
A. It's on a speaker-disjoint test, which is the guard against that. The honest
   caveat is the unseen-generator gap: 17% against a never-seen voice-conversion
   model. Seen-generator performance is strong; truly novel generators are the open
   problem, and we say so.


================================================================================
D. ROBUSTNESS & ATTACKS
================================================================================

Q. What happens over a real phone line / codec?
A. Telephony is our STRONGEST channel. AMR-NB (8 kHz) recall ~100%, MP3 @ 16 kbit/s
   ~92%. For many published detectors MP3@16k pushes EER from ~3.7% to ~55%; ours
   barely moves. That matters because the phone codec is exactly our deployment
   condition.

Q. Can't an attacker just add reverb or noise to evade you?
A. That's "audio laundering," and it was our weak spot — heavy reverb dropped recall
   to ~13–18%. We fixed it by training THROUGH those degradations (augmentation):
   reverb recall roughly tripled to ~39–44%, and 10 dB-noise recall rose to ~82%.
   We attack ourselves with our own laundering script, so we measure this honestly.

Q. What about noise suppression like Krisp or Teams?
A. Double-edged: aggressive denoising strips the artifacts we rely on and can make
   REAL speech look fake. Two defenses. (1) We detect enhancement and halve our own
   confidence when we see it, so it can't reach HOLD. (2) We trained through a
   noise-suppression simulation, which dropped the denoising false-positive rate to
   6.7% — actually below our clean baseline of 8.7%.

Q. What's the hardest attack?
A. Real-time voice conversion (e.g. Seed-VC) — it converts a live speaker into a
   target voice and defeats prosody-based defenses. It's the attack we demo
   ourselves, and it's where the unseen-generator gap (17%) shows up. We're honest
   that novel VC is the frontier.

Q. Why several clone generators in training, not one?
A. The failure mode of this field is synthesis-method mismatch: a detector that has
   only seen one generator learns THAT generator's fingerprint, not "fakeness." We
   train across 9 generators for breadth; adding more is the roadmap.

Q. Can an attacker just play a real recording (replay)?
A. Replay of genuine audio is a different threat (the audio IS real). The spectral/
   codec detector picks up channel and playback artifacts, and the context engine
   still applies (unverified caller, large amount). Full anti-replay is a known
   extension.


================================================================================
E. LATENCY & DEPLOYMENT
================================================================================

Q. Is it actually real-time?
A. Yes. On our demo laptop a 4-second window is processed in ~669 ms — a real-time
   factor of 0.167, with ~330 ms of headroom inside the 1-second hop. First verdict
   ~4.4 seconds into the call, refreshing about every second.

Q. Your export says "OVER BUDGET 3.4 s" — explain.
A. That's the single-thread INT8 self-check — a deliberate worst case. The real,
   multi-threaded number from our benchmark is 669 ms. We always quote the measured
   multi-thread figure.

Q. Does it need a GPU?
A. No — it runs INT8 on CPU in production. We use a GPU only to make TRAINING fast
   (minutes). Banks won't put GPUs on call servers, so CPU inference was a hard
   requirement.

Q. How does it scale to many concurrent calls?
A. Each call is an independent stream; the model is 102 MB and ~0.17 RTF per core,
   so one core handles several calls or one call with large headroom. It scales
   horizontally with CPU cores/servers.

Q. What does "hop" and "window" mean?
A. We look at the last 4 seconds (window) and re-score every 1 second (hop). 4 s is
   the field standard — sub-second windows can't see the features that matter — and
   a 1 s hop keeps the meter live.


================================================================================
F. FALSE POSITIVES & THE EVIDENCE GATE
================================================================================

Q. Do you guarantee zero false positives?
A. No — no detector can, and we don't claim it. Our job is to MINIMIZE them. Under
   aggressive noise suppression our false-positive rate is 6.7% and we halve
   confidence when we detect enhancement so it can't reach HOLD; and the evidence
   gate ensures silence/noise never scores at all. The system holds and advises — a
   human officer makes the final decision, so a false positive costs a verification
   step, not a cancelled transaction.

Q. What is the evidence gate?
A. Before scoring, a window must contain real speech. Three checks: dynamic range
   >= 12 dB (real speech varies 20–40 dB; steady noise < 6 dB), at least 2.5 s of
   net speech out of 4, and SNR/packet-loss sanity. If it fails, we ABSTAIN —
   "insufficient evidence" — instead of guessing. This is what makes the system
   safe to deploy: it never false-alarms on silence, hold music, or room noise.

Q. Why does it sometimes say "insufficient evidence" while I'm talking?
A. Natural speech has pauses, so some 4-second windows dip below the 2.5 s speech
   floor, and the gate abstains for that window. The UI holds the last verdict for
   up to 6 seconds through these brief dips, so it doesn't flicker; sustained
   silence correctly falls back to "waiting for speech."

Q. Why not just always output a score?
A. Because a score on silence or noise is a lie, and one false hold on a real
   customer destroys trust in the whole system. Abstaining is the honest and
   deployable choice.


================================================================================
G. THE DECISION / FUSION
================================================================================

Q. How do you combine the detectors?
A. Three independent signals — neural (weight 0.55), spectral/codec (0.25), speaker
   trajectory (0.20) — are fused in log-odds, then Platt-calibrated. A context lift
   adds transaction amount, fraud-risk tier, caller-ID status, and scam keywords.

Q. Why not just use the neural net alone?
A. Defense in depth. Each detector fails differently: the neural net is weak on
   unseen generators, the spectral detector catches high-frequency collapse the
   neural net might miss, and the speaker detector catches mid-call voice switching.
   Fusing them covers each other's blind spots.

Q. What stops the meter from over-reacting to one bad window?
A. Two things: an exponential moving average (alpha 0.6) smooths the score, and
   K-of-N persistence requires elevated confidence in at least 3 of the last 6
   windows before escalating to HOLD. One anomalous window cannot hold a transfer.

Q. What are the bands?
A. SAFE (no action), WATCH (flag, keep watching), VERIFY (officer step-up
   challenge), HOLD (hold the transaction; human decides). We advise and hold — we
   never auto-cancel.


================================================================================
H. PRIVACY, AUDIT, COMPLIANCE
================================================================================

Q. Do you store people's calls?
A. No raw audio is written to disk — only extracted numeric features and hashes.
   That's a deliberate privacy and compliance decision for banking.

Q. How is it auditable?
A. Every event — each scored frame, each alert, each officer override — is appended
   to a tamper-evident SHA-256 hash chain: each entry hashes the previous one, so
   any later tampering is detectable. It's the verifiable ledger a bank audit needs,
   and it's viewable live in the dashboard.

Q. Is this biometric data? Any consent issues?
A. We process the voice transiently to score it and keep only derived features and
   hashes, not the voiceprint or the audio. Deployment would follow the bank's
   existing call-recording consent and data-protection policies.


================================================================================
I. THE DEMO / PRODUCT
================================================================================

Q. Show me it working.
A. Start a live call, allow the mic, speak normally — the meter reads SAFE
   ("legitimate voice"). Then play a cloned clip (or run the Seed-VC conversion)
   into the mic — within a few seconds it climbs to WATCH/VERIFY, and the reasons
   panel explains why. Real speech stays SAFE; fakes escalate.

Q. What if the demo breaks / no internet / no backend?
A. There's a mock server that serves the same routes with fixture data, so the
   dashboard runs unchanged. And the Audio File Lab analyzes a pre-recorded .wav if
   the live mic misbehaves.

Q. Light and dark mode?
A. Light is the default; a header toggle switches to dark and remembers the choice.
   The design is a "signal instrument" look — clean forensic panels, a live
   waveform, an indigo->cyan accent.

Q. Why does the first verdict take ~4–5 seconds?
A. It needs a full 4-second window of mostly-speech before it will commit to a
   score — that's the evidence gate being honest rather than guessing early on one
   syllable.


================================================================================
J. LIMITATIONS & ROADMAP (say these before you're asked)
================================================================================

Q. Where does it fail?
A. Novel, never-seen voice-conversion models (~17% EER) are the hard case — the
   field's open problem. Heavy reverb and 0 dB-SNR noise still degrade recall,
   though augmentation tripled reverb recall. Real telephony channels — our actual
   deployment — are our strongest conditions, which is the point.

Q. What's next?
A. Re-measure leave-one-generator-out on the deployed v4 head; add more generators
   (vits, more ASVspoof systems) for breadth; broaden Hindi/Indic coverage; swap
   the dependency-free VAD for silero-vad; add speaker enrollment for full
   verification.

Q. Why should this win / what's novel?
A. Three things almost nobody ships together: (1) an evidence gate that abstains
   instead of false-alarming, (2) honest, speaker-disjoint, language-matched
   evaluation with the unseen-generator gap reported openly, and (3) real-time CPU
   inference with a tamper-evident audit chain — a system a bank could actually
   deploy, not a leaderboard number.

Q. Is the model trained or is this a mock?
A. Trained and deployed. The neural detector is a real 101.3M-parameter model with
   a trained head; model_card.json says "trained": true. Day 1 used stubs so the
   whole pipeline ran end-to-end; those have been replaced by real detectors.
